Principal Characters

The principal figures of the saga, arranged by kindred and affiliation.

The House of Biarg

Grettir Asmundson
Called the Strong (Grettir sterki); the hero of the saga, renowned for his extraordinary strength and courage.
Asmund Thorgrimson
Called the Greyhaired (hærulángr); Grettir’s father, a chieftain of Biarg in Midfirth.
Asdis
Bard Jokulson’s daughter; Grettir’s mother, a woman of strong character and resolve.
Atli Asmundson
Grettir’s elder brother; a peaceable and prosperous farmer.
Illugi Asmundson
Grettir’s younger brother, who shares his exile on Drangey.
Thorstein Asmundson
Called Dromund; Grettir’s brother, who journeys abroad after Grettir’s outlawry.

Ancestors and Settlers

Onund Ufeigh’s son
Called Treefoot (tréfótr); Grettir’s grandfather, a warrior who settles in Iceland after the battle of Hafrsfirth.
Ufeigh Grettir
Grettir’s great-grandfather (Úfeigr), a powerful chieftain and lawman, first settler of Ufeigh’s-firth.
Ufeigh Einarson
Called Grettir; the grandfather from whom the hero takes his name.
Bard Jokulson
Grettir’s maternal grandfather, of the stock of the men of Sea-dales.
Aud the Deeply Wealthy
Auðr djúpauðga; a renowned settler, ancestress of the line of Biarg.

Neighbours and Chieftains

Thorbiörn Thordson
Called Angle; a wealthy chieftain of Willowdale and Grettir’s principal adversary.
Thorbiörn Arnorson
Called Oxmain (öxnamegin); a powerful and quarrelsome neighbour in Midfirth.
Thorfinn Karrson
Of Haramsey; a good and generous man, a friend to Grettir in times of need.
Thorkel Krafla
Þorkell Krafla; a chieftain of the north who offers Grettir shelter.
Thorgils Arison
Of Reek-knolls; a notable chieftain and friend to Grettir in the north country.
Gudmund the Rich
hinn ríki; of Maddervales, one of the most powerful chieftains in Iceland.
Skapti Thorodson
Of Hjalli; the Lawman of Iceland, one of the wisest men of his age.
Snorri Thorgrimson
Called Snorri Godi; the renowned chieftain of Helgafell.
Kormak of Meal
A yeoman of Midfirth; a violent and troublesome neighbour.

Kings and Earls

King Olaf the Saint
Óláfr Haraldsson helgi; King of Norway, in whose court Grettir seeks justice.
Earl Svein
Hákonarson; Earl of Norway, whom Grettir serves during the wars.
Harald Halfdanson
Called the Fair-hair (hárfagri); King of Norway, whose battle of Hafrsfirth opens the saga’s genealogy.
Magnus the Good
King of Norway in the latter days of the saga.
Eric Hakonson
Earl of Norway; a patron of skalds.

Women of the Saga

Thurid
Thorbiörn Angle’s stepmother, a woman skilled in old lore.
Thorbiorg the Big
Þorbjörg digra; Olaf’s daughter, a wise woman of Spákonufell.
Steinvor of Sand-heaps
A stalwart woman who shelters outlaws in the wilderness.
Spes
The wife of Sigurd, a woman of Constantinople whose story intertwines with Thorstein Dromund’s.
Asta Gudbrand’s-daughter
Mother of King Olaf the Saint.

Skalds and Outlaws

Thord Yeller
gellir; a poet, son of Hallmund the mountain sprite.
Thormod Coalbrowskald
kolbrúnarskáld; a poet and warrior.
Thorgeir Bottleback
flöskubak; a turbulent outlaw of the heaths.
Bardi Gudmundson
Called Slaying-Bardi (Víga-Bardi); of Asbiornsness.
Thorbiörn Noise
glaumr; Grettir’s servant-man.

Spirits and the Supernatural

Glam
A huge and surly Swedish shepherd from Sylgsdales.
Hallmund
A mountain sprite (bergrisi), friend to Grettir in the wilderness, skilled in poetry.
Karr the Old
A ghost dwelling in a mound on Haramsey.
Thorir of Thorirs-dale
A mountain-sprite (landvættir) of the interior.
